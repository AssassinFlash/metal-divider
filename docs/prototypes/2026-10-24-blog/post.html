Open Area Explained: Choosing Perforation Density for Privacy, Light and Airflow

MetalDivider Team Sep 15, 2026 7 min read

Open area is the single number that most changes how a metal screen behaves. Across the 146 patterns in our catalog, open area ranges from 6.0% to 85.48%, with a median of 35.0% — and every step along that range trades privacy against light, airflow, and visual weight. This guide explains what the number means, how to read it, and how to choose the right band for your project.

What is open area?

Open area is the percentage of a panel's surface that is cut away — holes, slots, or laser-cut openings — relative to the total panel area. A panel with 40% open area is 40% open space and 60% solid metal. It is calculated from the pattern geometry: for perforated patterns, from hole size and pitch (the center-to-center hole spacing); for laser-cut patterns, from the drawn cut lines themselves.

Two patterns can look similar at a glance and differ by 30 percentage points of open area. That difference decides whether a screen reads as a wall or as a veil — which is why open area, not the motif, is the first specification an experienced buyer asks about.

What open-area ranges do screen patterns come in?

Our pattern library spans 6.0% to 85.48% open area. In practice, patterns cluster into three working bands:

How does open area affect privacy?

Privacy tracks open area, but not perfectly — hole size matters as much as percentage. At the catalog median of 35.0%, silhouettes blur beyond a few meters while daylight passes freely. Below 20%, a screen reads as near-solid from most viewing angles. Above roughly 60%, treat the screen as a visual texture rather than a privacy barrier.

Hole geometry explains the exceptions: a pattern with many small perforations can reach a high open-area figure while still diffusing detail, while a pattern with a few large openings at the same percentage shows whatever stands behind it. When privacy is the priority, specify small holes, mid open area — the combination the perforated series in our catalog is built around.

How does open area change light and airflow?

Light transmission and airflow both scale roughly with the open fraction. A 35% open panel admits about a third of the incident light; at 85% open area, the panel is closer to a frame than a filter. In interior projects that difference sets how a space feels behind the screen — from dappled and intimate to bright and connected.

Outdoors, higher open area also lets wind pass through rather than push on the panel, which reduces the load the fixings have to carry. And the shadow a screen casts is a design feature in its own right: the denser the pattern, the stronger the projected geometry on floors and walls — a frequent reason lobby and facade projects choose mid-band patterns over open ones.

How do I specify open area when ordering?

Three numbers define a perforated pattern: hole geometry (diameter or slot size), pitch (hole spacing), and the resulting open area. For laser-cut patterns, open area follows from the artwork. To keep a specification tight:

  • Start from the effect you want — privacy, daylight, shadow pattern — and work back to a band, not a single number.
  • Fine holes at tight pitch give an even texture; large openings give a graphic pattern.
  • Confirm the open area on the pattern specification before production; every value is confirmed per drawing.
  • For exterior installations, mention the mounting context when you inquire — open area interacts with wind behavior and the fixing plan.

Frequently asked questions

What open area is best for privacy screens?

Most privacy applications land between 20% and 45%. The catalog median of 35.0% is a reliable starting point: outlines blur at conversation distance, daylight passes, and the screen keeps its visual weight low.

Does a higher open area weaken the panel?

Stiffness depends on the combination of open area, pattern geometry, material, and sheet thickness — not on open area alone. Structural requirements are reviewed and confirmed per drawing before production.

Can open area be customized?

Yes. Catalog patterns cover 6.0% to 85.48%, and custom patterns are cut from your drawing — the open area follows the artwork.

How is open area calculated?

Open area = total area of all openings ÷ total panel area × 100. For perforated patterns it derives from hole size and pitch; for laser-cut patterns, from the cut geometry.
