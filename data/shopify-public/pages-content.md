What drives the cost of custom stainless steel screens?
Custom stainless screen pricing is shaped by material grade, thickness, pattern density, finish, quantity, and hardware detail.
A simple per-square-meter number can be misleading until these variables are known.
Material grade
- 316 costs more than 304 but may be the correct choice for coastal or poolside exposure.
Thickness and frame
- taller panels, large spans, and exterior use often need heavier sheet or reinforced framing.
Pattern density
- fine cuts, tight bridges, and high detail increase cutting time and finishing work.
Finish
- brushed stainless is usually simpler; PVD, antique, mirror, and custom color finishes add processing steps.
Quantity
- repeated panels reduce setup cost per unit; one-off feature walls carry more engineering time.
A budget quote should include the screen type, grade, thickness, finish, panel dimensions, mounting approach, and packing method. For procurement teams, this makes quote comparison clearer than a loose material-only estimate.

What stainless steel grades do you use?
304 stainless steel works for most interior screens, partitions, wall panels, and protected exterior areas. 316 stainless steel is better for coastal, marine, poolside, and high-humidity environments.
Send the installation location and exposure level with your drawing. We will recommend the grade before quoting.
What is the difference between 304 and 316 stainless steel for architectural screens?
304 and 316 are the two main stainless grades for custom screens; the specification choice comes down to exposure, not appearance.
304 stainless is the practical baseline for controlled interiors and many inland projects. 316 stainless adds molybdenum, which improves resistance to chloride exposure from salt air, pool chemicals, and aggressive cleaning regimes.

Indoor and outdoor screens can look similar in renders, but the specification should change for exposure, fixing, and long-term maintenance.
Interior screens usually focus on finish quality, sightlines, and integration with ceilings or millwork. Exterior screens also need weather, wind, drainage, and corrosion control.

How durable are PVD finishes on stainless steel screens?
PVD finish is a thin metal coating bonded to stainless steel in a vacuum process, used when the project needs color with a metallic surface rather than paint coverage.
It is common for champagne, bronze, black titanium, rose gold, and gunmetal stainless screens.
Choose the base texture
- hairline, brushed, mirror, bead-blasted, or etched texture changes the final reflection.
Choose the color family
- champagne and bronze feel warmer; black titanium and gunmetal feel quieter and more architectural.
Match the environment
- exterior or humid projects should pair the finish with the correct stainless grade and cleaning plan.
PVD is well suited to hotel lobbies, restaurant screens, feature walls, and glass-hybrid dividers because it gives color while preserving the stainless substrate. High-touch public areas should use samples to check fingerprint visibility, reflection, and cleaning tolerance under the actual lighting.
Request finish swatches before final approval. The same PVD color can read very differently under warm hotel lighting, daylight, and wall-wash fixtures.
Do metal partitions show fingerprints, and how should they be cleaned?
For high-touch zones, we recommend hairline finishes (naturally better fingerprint resistance) or optional PVD anti-fingerprint coating (AFP). Daily cleaning is simple: use a soft damp cloth. For tougher stains, use a mild cleaner and avoid abrasive pads or scouring products.
Will PVD color coatings fade in interior environments?
No. PVD (Physical Vapor Deposition) is not standard paint; it forms a bonded coating layer on the metal surface. In normal interior use, PVD finishes can last more than 15 years without fading or peeling.
How do I maintain stainless steel screens after installation?
Maintenance depends on the grade, finish, and environment, but most stainless screens need simple routine cleaning rather than heavy upkeep.
The goal is to remove dust, fingerprints, salt residue, and site contamination before they sit on the surface for long periods.
Interior screens
- wipe with a soft cloth and pH-neutral cleaner; follow the grain direction on brushed finishes.
Exterior screens
- rinse and clean more often in coastal, poolside, or dusty environments.
PVD finishes
- use non-abrasive cloths and mild cleaners to protect the colored surface.
Hardware checks
- review exposed fasteners, base plates, and drainage areas during scheduled facility maintenance.
Avoid abrasive pads and chloride-heavy cleaners, especially on colored or brushed finishes. For exterior 316 stainless screens, regular freshwater rinsing can help preserve appearance near salt air.
Ask for a maintenance note matched to the selected grade and finish before shipment, so the handover package is ready for the owner or facility team.

Why PVD outlasts outdoor paint and powder coat:
Molecular bonding
— PVD is vapor-deposited at the atomic level into the steel surface, not a layer sitting on top. It doesn't peel, chip, or delaminate.
UV-stable pigments
— mineral-based PVD color resists photo-bleach. Outdoor paint fades in 3–5 years; PVD holds 10–15.
304 / 316L base metal
— the substrate itself resists corrosion. Even with a surface scratch, a nick reveals more stainless, not steel that rusts.

Why this matters for landscape-adjacent installs:
Architects ask for the Corten aesthetic without the Corten problem. Corten patinas to a warm rust-brown that pairs beautifully with stone — until the first heavy rain washes iron oxide onto Italian marble, travertine coping, and poured concrete. The stain is permanent without chemical treatment.
Bronze PVD on 304 stainless delivers the same warm patina with zero runoff risk. The PVD layer is a vapor-deposited titanium-bronze ceramic surface — inert, with no free iron to oxidize. The aesthetic stays; the runoff goes to zero.

What buyers ask before ordering.
What is the difference between 304 and 316 stainless steel for architectural screens?
304 and 316 are the two main stainless grades for custom screens; the specification choice comes down to exposure, not appearance.
304 stainless is the practical baseline for controlled interiors and many inland projects. 316 stainless adds molybdenum, which improves resistance to chloride exposure from salt air, pool chemicals, and aggressive cleaning regimes.
Factor
304 Stainless Steel
316 Stainless Steel
Best environment
Interior, dry hospitality, office, retail, inland exterior
Coastal, poolside, spa, marine-adjacent, high-humidity exterior
Cost position
Baseline material choice
Premium grade for exposure control
Common screen use
Room dividers, lobby panels, decorative partitions
Facade screens, landscape privacy screens, resort exteriors
Finish pairing
Brushed, mirror, PVD, powder coat
PVD, powder coat, brushed, passivated exterior finishes
Use 304
when the screen is indoors or protected from chloride exposure.
Use 316
when the screen sits near salt air, pools, spas, or exterior weather.
Send the project location, indoor/outdoor condition, and cleaning environment with the inquiry. The quote can then lock the grade, finish, and maintenance expectation before samples are made.
Which stainless screen type should I choose for my project?

A parent page should not force every buyer into one product format; the project condition should point to the right route.

Laser-cut sheets offer the most direct path for flat panels and repeat patterns. Glass-hybrid systems add a second material layer when partial enclosure, reflection, or light diffusion matters. Outdoor screens need grade, frame, drainage, and fixing details checked as one system.
Share the scene, size, and desired privacy level. The team can route the project to the right stainless screen format before quoting.
What drives the cost of custom stainless steel screens?
Custom stainless screen pricing is shaped by material grade, thickness, pattern density, finish, quantity, and hardware detail.
A simple per-square-meter number can be misleading until these variables are known.
Material grade
- 316 costs more than 304 but may be the correct choice for coastal or poolside exposure.
Thickness and frame
- taller panels, large spans, and exterior use often need heavier sheet or reinforced framing.
Pattern density
- fine cuts, tight bridges, and high detail increase cutting time and finishing work.
Finish
- brushed stainless is usually simpler; PVD, antique, mirror, and custom color finishes add processing steps.
Quantity
- repeated panels reduce setup cost per unit; one-off feature walls carry more engineering time.
A budget quote should include the screen type, grade, thickness, finish, panel dimensions, mounting approach, and packing method. For procurement teams, this makes quote comparison clearer than a loose material-only estimate.
Send dimensions, quantity, finish target, and installation location to receive a project-specific price band.
Should indoor and outdoor stainless screens be specified differently?
Indoor and outdoor screens can look similar in renders, but the specification should change for exposure, fixing, and long-term maintenance.
Interior screens usually focus on finish quality, sightlines, and integration with ceilings or millwork. Exterior screens also need weather, wind, drainage, and corrosion control.

Include climate, site photos, and mounting surface details when asking for an outdoor stainless screen quote.
How durable are PVD finishes on stainless steel screens?
PVD finish is a thin metal coating bonded to stainless steel in a vacuum process, used when the project needs color with a metallic surface rather than paint coverage.
It is common for champagne, bronze, black titanium, rose gold, and gunmetal stainless screens.
Choose the base texture
- hairline, brushed, mirror, bead-blasted, or etched texture changes the final reflection.
Choose the color family
- champagne and bronze feel warmer; black titanium and gunmetal feel quieter and more architectural.
Match the environment
- exterior or humid projects should pair the finish with the correct stainless grade and cleaning plan.
PVD is well suited to hotel lobbies, restaurant screens, feature walls, and glass-hybrid dividers because it gives color while preserving the stainless substrate. High-touch public areas should use samples to check fingerprint visibility, reflection, and cleaning tolerance under the actual lighting.
Request finish swatches before final approval. The same PVD color can read very differently under warm hotel lighting, daylight, and wall-wash fixtures.
How does open area affect privacy, light, and airflow?
Open area is one of the most important numbers in a stainless screen specification because it controls privacy, daylight, shadow, and ventilation.
It is the percentage of the panel surface removed by laser cutting, perforation, or spacing.

How should stainless screens coordinate with glass, stone, millwork, ceilings, and lighting?
A stainless screen should be coordinated as part of the surrounding architectural package, not treated as a loose decorative panel.
The cleanest installations usually happen when the screen joints, brackets, lighting cavities, and adjacent materials are discussed during shop drawing review.
Ceilings and floors
- confirm track, base plate, or concealed bracket positions before site finishes close.
Glass interfaces
- define glass thickness, edge profile, gasket detail, and frame tolerance early.
Stone and millwork
- align screen joints with reveals, door frames, wall panels, and counter edges.
Lighting
- backlit and wall-wash screens need cavity depth, access, diffuser choice, and finish direction checked together.
For hotel, restaurant, and commercial fit-out work, numbered panels and fixing drawings reduce site confusion. Packing labels should match the installation sequence so contractors can move from crate to location without relabeling.
Send floor plans, reflected ceiling plans, elevations, and finish schedules with the inquiry for a cleaner shop drawing package.

How do I maintain stainless steel screens after installation?
Maintenance depends on the grade, finish, and environment, but most stainless screens need simple routine cleaning rather than heavy upkeep.
The goal is to remove dust, fingerprints, salt residue, and site contamination before they sit on the surface for long periods.
Interior screens
- wipe with a soft cloth and pH-neutral cleaner; follow the grain direction on brushed finishes.
Exterior screens
- rinse and clean more often in coastal, poolside, or dusty environments.
PVD finishes
- use non-abrasive cloths and mild cleaners to protect the colored surface.
Hardware checks
- review exposed fasteners, base plates, and drainage areas during scheduled facility maintenance.
Avoid abrasive pads and chloride-heavy cleaners, especially on colored or brushed finishes. For exterior 316 stainless screens, regular freshwater rinsing can help preserve appearance near salt air.
Ask for a maintenance note matched to the selected grade and finish before shipment, so the handover package is ready for the owner or facility team.

Drawing &amp; Design
What file formats do you accept?
DXF, DWG, and PDF. DXF is preferred for fastest processing. If you only have a sketch or photo, send it — we can redraw it for DFM review.
What is the minimum feature size?
Minimum web width is 3mm. Holes should be at least 1.5× material thickness. We flag violations in the DFM report.
Can you cut from a hand sketch?
Yes. Send a photo or scan. We redraw to DXF and send back for approval before quoting.
Material &amp; Thickness
What grades of stainless steel do you cut?
304 and 316 are standard. 201 and 430 available on request. MTR provided for all grades.
What thickness range?
0.5mm to 6.0mm standard. Thicker plates possible with CO₂ laser or waterjet — we review case by case.
Do you supply material or do I?
We supply. All material sourced from verified mills with traceability certificates.
Surface Finish
What finishes are available?
Brushed (#4), mirror (8K), PVD color coating, and powder coating. Sample chips available on request.
Can you match an existing finish?
Yes. Send a sample or photo. We produce a match sample for approval before batch production.
Is the cut edge finished?
Yes. All edges are deburred and sharp points rounded. The cut face itself is clean — no secondary finishing needed for most applications.
Shipping &amp; Packaging
How are panels packaged?
Flat on wooden pallets with protective film on both sides. Edge protectors on request. Fumigation certificate for export.
What is the lead time?
Standard lead time is 15–20 days after drawing approval. Rush orders possible — ask.
Do you ship internationally?
Yes. FOB Shanghai or CIF to your port. Door-to-door available via DHL/UPS for small quantities.

What strand widths are available?
Standard strand widths run 1.5mm–6mm. Wider strands increase structural rigidity; narrower strands maximise the open area percentage.
Which finishes are available?
Mill finish, powder coat (RAL palette), anodised aluminium, or hot-dip galvanised for exterior use.
Can hexagonal mesh be curved?
Yes — hexagonal mesh bends across one axis more readily than diamond. Minimum bend radius depends on strand gauge and aperture size.
Does micro mesh carry acoustic backing?
We can laminate 25–50mm acoustic foam or mineral wool behind the mesh panel before framing. Typical NRC improvement: 0.4–0.7.
Is raised flat mesh code-compliant for balustrades?
Yes — when framed in a compliant balustrade system and using the correct gauge (3mm+ strand residential, 5mm+ commercial). We supply structural calculations on request.

What installation methods are commonly used for expanded metal panels in building facades, ceilings, and interior partitions?
Proper installation ensures structural integrity, alignment, and long-term performance. Most architects specify framed systems for ease of mounting and future maintenance.
Facade cladding:
Panels are mounted on extruded aluminum sub-frames using Z-clips, hook-on systems, or concealed brackets; fixed with stainless steel fasteners; designed with 10–20 mm joints for thermal expansion.
Ceilings:
Suspended on T-bar grids, lay-in framed panels, or swing-down access frames; must maintain minimum open area for sprinkler compliance; often combined with acoustic backing.
Interior partitions and balustrades:
Framed into steel or aluminum extrusions, bolted or welded to posts; tempered glass infills optional for hybrid designs.
All methods require coordination with structural engineers for wind loads (facades) and seismic requirements where applicable.

Can the pattern be customized?
Yes. We can adapt geometric, Islamic, floral, or brand patterns into a manufacturable laser-cut file.
Which stainless grade works outdoors?
Use 316 for coastal, humid, poolside, or high-exposure locations. 304 can work for protected exterior areas.
Can you match railing frames?
Yes. Share frame dimensions and finish direction so panel thickness, fixing holes, and finish path align early.
Do partitions need frames?
Most interior panels use perimeter frames for clean installation. Frameless options depend on size, thickness, and fixing method.
Can you provide samples?

Can holes be pre-cut?
Yes. Send the frame drawing and fixing plan so holes, slots, and edge offsets are cut in production.
What affects price most?
Material grade, panel thickness, pattern density, finish, and total quantity drive most of the cost.
Can you work from a photo?

Can both sides look finished?
Yes. We can specify finish and fabrication details so the gate reads cleanly from both directions.
How are large panels packed?
Panels are film-protected, separated with EPE foam, labeled, and packed in reinforced plywood crates.

Yes. We review joint rhythm and pattern repeat in the production drawing before cutting.

Frequently Asked Questions
What stainless steel grades do you use?
304 stainless steel works for most interior screens, partitions, wall panels, and protected exterior areas. 316 stainless steel is better for coastal, marine, poolside, and high-humidity environments.
Send the installation location and exposure level with your drawing. We will recommend the grade before quoting.
What finishes are available?
Common finishes include brushed hairline, mirror, bead blast, satin, vibration, etched surfaces, powder coat, and PVD colors.

Can I use my own pattern design?
Yes. Send a sketch, CAD file, DXF, AI file, photo, or reference image. We convert it into a manufacturable pattern and return a production drawing for approval.
Simple adaptations of existing patterns are usually handled inside the quote. Fully custom engineering is confirmed before we begin.
What is the minimum order quantity?
Standard patterns usually start from 30 sqm. Custom patterns usually start from 50 sqm. Sample panels can be made before production so you can confirm material, finish, and pattern scale.
How do I get a quote?
Send the application, approximate dimensions, quantity, preferred material, finish, and any drawings or reference images. We reply with method recommendation, production notes, lead time, and pricing.
How long does production take?
Samples usually take 7–12 working days. Standard production usually takes 15–20 working days. Custom finishes, larger quantities, and complex fabrication can take longer.
Do you ship worldwide?
Yes. We ship by sea freight or air freight depending on timeline and order size. Panels are protected with film, separated with EPE foam, labeled, and packed in reinforced plywood crates.
